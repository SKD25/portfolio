Flight Sciences Intern

What: Contributing to the Flight Sciences development of hybrid regional aircraft by conducting performance analyses and automating trade study workfklow.

Why: To develop a more sustainable method of regional air travel, and gain experience as an engineer within the conceptual design phase of the aerospace industry.

How: As a Flight Sciences intern, among other tasks I primarily help automate our team's trade study capabilities and conduct performance analyses to ensure our aircraft design can meet customer requirements.

To automate our team's trade study capabilities, I developed a Python-based tool that generates geometries in OpenVSP, exports a Plot3D format of the design, creates a mesh in FlightStream with control surfaces (a Siemens panel-based product that empirically models flow separation and frictional effects), automatically sets boundary conditions, and initializes the solver. Using this workflow, I conducted a fuselage-diameter trade study, finding a 5% increase in drag, and 2% associated decrease in range for the larger fuselage. I presented these findings to a technical review board.

I also assist with conducting performance analyses for reference missions to determine our sensitivity on the all-electric range capabilities of our design using an Aviary-based tool. Examples of such sensitivity studies include the effect of battery aging or pessimistic drag polars on our all-electric range.

To assist with the customer-facing side, I created a headwind estimator based on historical data in the airline's typical region of operation, which provides monthly estimates of the average headwind at 10000 feet altitude when traveling from one airport to another. This tool was used to modify the conditions for the performance analyses that were conducted to verify our design can meet an airline's intended schedule with ease.

I am very excited to continue working at this position until the end of Q4 for 2026!
